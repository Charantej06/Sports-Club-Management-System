*CHAMPIONS CLUB — FULL PROJECT EXPLANATION*
Updated: 3 October 2026

*What are we building?*
Champions Club is a sports club management system for tennis, padel, badminton and cricket. Customers can explore the club, create an account, buy a membership, use a digital member card, book courts and shop. Staff use role-specific screens for reception, cashier, kitchen and owner tasks, including clubhouse orders and financial administration.

This is a web application backed by PostgreSQL. Plans, products, members, orders and invoices are saved as database records. It is more than a set of static design pages.

*WHAT HAS BEEN COMPLETED AND VERIFIED?*

1. Public website: responsive home page, four interactive sport panels, facilities, membership previews, shop products, clubhouse information, trial-session entry point and enquiry form. Separate pages exist for courts, memberships, shop, clubhouse and accounts. Public prices, products, menu and availability come from the database.

2. Accounts: signup, login, logout, email verification, password reset, expiring sessions and unique Champions IDs. Customers can edit their profile and see their own membership, statement and receipts. Staff permissions are controlled on the server. Public signup cannot grant a staff role.

3. Memberships: Gold, Silver and Junior plans. The owner can change prices and benefits. Customers can purchase, renew or change a plan in local demo payment mode. Junior eligibility checks age. Each term stores its paid price and benefit snapshot, so a later plan edit does not change an old sale. Renewal history is preserved.

4. Digital member card: active members see a QR card. Its QR value is an opaque identifier rather than a name or personal details. Staff can resolve it to permitted member information. The card can be revoked and reissued. People without an active membership see a joining or renewal prompt.

5. Billing foundation: itemized invoices, payments, allocations, credits, refunds, outstanding balances and printable receipts. Membership purchase and its invoice/payment are saved together. Duplicate checkout requests are protected by an idempotency key. Amounts are calculated on the server in integer paise.

6. Staff foundation and enquiries: role-aware staff desk, owner settings/plan editing, member lookup, saved website enquiries and immediate front-desk notifications.

7. Local setup: database migrations, realistic demo data, local email inbox, optional SMTP configuration, separate background worker, Docker Compose files, environment example, CI configuration and setup instructions. The initial catalogue has 36 products; the clubhouse menu has 14 items.

The current local application passed lint, TypeScript, four unit tests, 43 real-PostgreSQL/HTTP integration tests and a production build. Desktop and mobile flows were checked in a browser. Docker Compose, physical camera/LAN devices, real SMTP and Razorpay acceptance were not execution-tested on the development host.

*STAGE 2 AND 3 OPERATIONS*

These operations are implemented and covered by local verification:

• Court booking: one-hour slots, opening hours and booking window, member/guest/trial pricing, temporary holds, confirmation, cancellation, check-in, maintenance closures, two sessions per member per club day, and history.
• Social play: Friday events, participant capacity, joining, check-in, waiting lists and expiring offers.
• Shop: cart, pickup/delivery checkout, shared inventory for online and counter sales, stock reservations, collection/dispatch/delivery, returns and stock movements.
• Clubhouse: table-based waiter/cashier POS, member or guest bills, item notes and additions, kitchen tickets with preparation states, member tabs, settlement and reasoned adjustments.
• Reception and CRM: member lookup, booking help, lead assignment, notes, follow-up reminders, quotes and lead conversion.
• Shared operations billing: court, shop and clubhouse invoices, receipts, partial payments, credits and refunds.

Owner reports include date filters, departmental sales, collections by method, refunds, balances, supporting records, exports, utilization and alerts. Administration includes business invoices/quotes, cash reconciliation, employees, shifts, leave, payslips, configured withholding and audits. Membership reminders run at configurable 7/1/0-day offsets through durable jobs, with local inbox, delivery status and retries. A Razorpay adapter verifies signatures and captures with duplicate-safe processing, but it has not been tested with actual provider credentials. Local payments remain explicitly simulated.

*HOW DOES THE SYSTEM WORK?*

A customer or staff member uses a Next.js web page. The page calls a server API. The API checks the session and permission, validates the input, then calls a business service. The service applies club rules and saves changes to PostgreSQL through Prisma. If something must happen later, such as an email or expiring hold, a durable job is saved and a separate worker processes it.

Example: customer requests a court -> server checks the customer, time, court, quota and price -> database reserves the slot for a short time -> customer confirms -> invoice and booking are recorded -> account history updates. A browser display alone is never proof that the slot is still available.

*TECH STACK — POSSIBLE QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS*

Q: Why Next.js and React?
A: React provides reusable interactive components for booking, cart, account and staff screens. Next.js App Router keeps pages and HTTP endpoints in one project, which a small team can build and explain without separate frontend and backend deployments. Server-rendered public pages also help initial loading and navigation.

Q: Why TypeScript?
A: It catches many mismatched data shapes and missing cases before the app runs. That matters because invoices, bookings, roles and order states pass through several services. We use strict TypeScript, but still validate requests at runtime because browser input cannot be trusted.

Q: Why PostgreSQL instead of static JSON or browser storage?
A: Bookings, stock and payments must persist and be shared by all devices. PostgreSQL provides transactions, locks and constraints that can protect scarce resources when two customers act at the same moment. Browser storage cannot coordinate that safely.

Q: Why Prisma?
A: Prisma maps database tables to typed application models, making normal reads and writes easier to maintain. Where correctness needs specific PostgreSQL features, the project also uses SQL migrations and explicit locks. PostgreSQL remains the source of truth.

Q: Why a modular monolith instead of microservices?
A: Membership, booking, shop and billing share customers and atomic transactions. One application reduces network complexity and makes cross-department rules easier to enforce. The code is still separated into business modules, and delayed work runs in a separate worker.

Q: Why Better Auth?
A: Password hashing, sessions, verification and resets have difficult security details. Better Auth supplies supported authentication flows. Our application adds club-specific role and record-ownership checks.

Q: Why Zod if TypeScript already exists?
A: TypeScript types disappear at runtime. Zod checks real incoming values such as quantities, dates and action names before they reach business logic.

Q: Why React Query and React Hook Form?
A: React Query helps refresh changing server data and show loading/error states. React Hook Form manages interactive forms. The server still decides whether an action is allowed and what it costs.

Q: Why a separate worker?
A: Emails, hold expiry and waiting-list offers should continue if a customer closes a page or the app restarts. Jobs are stored in the database and processed with retry behavior.

*COMPUTER SCIENCE / SYSTEM DESIGN QUESTIONS*

Q: What is a database transaction?
A: It groups related changes so they all succeed or all roll back. A membership sale must save its membership term, invoice, payment allocation and checkout result together. We do not want an invoice without the membership it paid for.

Q: What is a race condition here?
A: It is when two requests read the same old state and both try to act on it. Two customers may choose the same court; two buyers may request the last product. The operations code uses transactions, row locks and database constraints so only a valid result can commit.

Q: Why use database constraints if the API already checks availability?
A: The API's answer can become stale between checking and saving. A database constraint is the final guard, including when requests arrive simultaneously or a different code path writes to the same table. Overlapping active reservations are protected at database level.

Q: What does idempotency mean?
A: Repeating a request with the same key and payload returns the original result instead of charging or reserving twice. Reusing that key with a different payload is rejected. This handles double-clicks and network retries.

Q: Why store money as integer paise?
A: Floating-point numbers can create small rounding errors. Rs 100.50 becomes exactly 10050 paise. Discounts use an explicit rounding rule, and the browser never supplies the authoritative price.

Q: Why save timestamps in UTC but use Asia/Kolkata club days?
A: UTC gives one consistent stored instant. Opening hours, daily booking limits and weekly benefits follow the club's local calendar. Keeping these ideas separate avoids date-boundary errors.

Q: What is authentication versus authorization?
A: Authentication answers, “Who are you?” Authorization answers, “May you do this or see this record?” A signed-in member still cannot open someone else's invoice. Both checks run on protected server routes.

Q: Why is the QR value an opaque token?
A: QR cards can be photographed. An opaque, revocable identifier exposes less personal information and can be invalidated if lost. It identifies a member for staff lookup; it is not payment approval.

Q: Why keep historical snapshots?
A: Prices and benefits can change tomorrow, but yesterday's invoice must still show what was sold then. Confirmed prices and benefits are saved at transaction time and historical records remain intact.

Q: How do we prevent overselling stock?
A: Online and counter orders use the same inventory. The service locks product variants, checks available stock, reserves quantities and rolls back the checkout if an item is unavailable. Fulfillment and returns write stock movements.

Q: Why create a temporary booking hold?
A: It gives a customer a short checkout window while preventing the same court from being sold twice. If checkout is abandoned, an expiry job releases the hold. Confirmation checks the relevant rules again.

Q: How is the system tested?
A: Unit tests cover isolated rules. The four unit tests and 43 integration tests passed, using real PostgreSQL for permissions, ownership, duplicate checkout, overlapping bookings, quotas, stock races, refunds, reports, cash reconciliation and worker retries. Browser checks covered customer and staff pages on desktop and mobile. Provider requests in gateway tests were mocked.
